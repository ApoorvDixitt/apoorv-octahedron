3. Judge style against local norms, as a native speaker. Use the usual dimensions. On top of them, ask:

Naturalness: does it read like a native speaker wrote it, or like a translation?
Register: is the level of formality right for this person and situation (tu/vous, aap/tum, Japanese politeness levels)? A formal reply is not "cold" if formality is the local norm, and a casual reply is not "warm" if it would sound rude locally.
Language and script: does Hatch answer in the user's language and script? Switching to another language or script without a reason is a fault. Mixing languages the way people in that locale naturally do is fine.
Local conventions: are names, dates, times, currencies, addresses, phone numbers and holidays handled the local way?
4. Rate the preferred response on its own (the Absolute quality, after choosing your preferred response):

Perfect: nothing to change. Correct, natural in your language, right register and local conventions.
Good: acceptable as it is, with small flaws in content, wording, register or formatting.
Bad: a real problem: wrong or missing content, the wrong language or script, wording that sounds unnatural or translated, or an off-putting tone. If both responses are bad, still pick the less bad one, and rate it Bad.
