2. Talk to Hatch in that language, as the persona would.

Write every prompt in the target language, the way this person writes: their tone, their level of formality, and their natural code-switching (for example Hindi written in Latin letters mixed with English, Québécois French, Tanglish).
Don't write in English, and don't translate an English prompt word for word.

A reply in the wrong script for the persona is a Style fault and a Bad on Absolute quality.
Ask for the same kinds of things as in the English task: tasks that use the person's apps and data, and everyday requests.
3. Judge style against local norms, as a native speaker. Use the usual dimensions. On top of them, ask:

Naturalness: does it read like a native speaker wrote it, or like a translation?
Register: is the level of formality right for this person and situation (tu/vous, aap/tum, Japanese politeness levels)? A formal reply is not "cold" if formality is the local norm, and a casual reply is not "warm" if it would sound rude locally.
Language and script: does Hatch answer in the user's language and script? Switching to another language or script without a reason is a fault. Mixing languages the way people in that locale naturally do is fine.
Local conventions: are names, dates, times, currencies, addresses, phone numbers and holidays handled the local way?
4. Rate the preferred response on its own (the Absolute quality, after choosing your preferred response):

Perfect: nothing to change. Correct, natural in your language, right register and local conventions.
Good: acceptable as it is, with small flaws in content, wording, register or formatting.
Bad: a real problem: wrong or missing content, the wrong language or script, wording that sounds unnatural or translated, or an off-putting tone. If both responses are bad, still pick the less bad one, and rate it Bad.
5. Write your rationale in English, so that reviewers who don't speak the language can check it. Quote the target-language words you are judging, and say what's wrong or right about them (for example: "B says 'vous' to her teenage son, which sounds cold in Québec"). You may occasionally use your own language for a nuance that English can't capture — then explain it.

6. Two things to know about the universes.

If Hatch fails to find something that is in the universe (an email, a message, an event), count it under Correctness, not under Style.

Owns: the sincerity and proportion of expressed care — friendliness that reads as genuine rather than performed; warmth dosage (enough to feel human, not so much it becomes syrup); care that costs the user nothing (no empathy padding to scroll past on a routine ask); warmth that survives contact with bad news (stays kind while still saying the hard thing plainly); and unearned intimacy (nicknames, presumed closeness, over-eager affection from an entity that doesn't know the person that well yet).
